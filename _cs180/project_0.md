## Part 1: Selfie: The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way
<!-- Row with 2 pictures -->
<div style="display: flex; gap: 15px; margin-bottom: 20px;">

When the camera is very close to the face, different facial features (nose, chin, ears) are at noticeably different distances from the lens, even though the differences are only a few centimeters away. Because perspective magnifies near objects far more than far objects, the nose (closest) appears disproportionately large relative to the ears and cheeks (farther away) — this is why close-up selfies exaggerate the nose and make the face look distorted. When you step back, the camera-to-face distance becomes large relative to the small differences in depth across the face. All facial features are almost the same distance from the camera. Their relative sizes stay proportional and the face looks natural. The picture that is taken further represents to how a person usually perceives faces when seeing someone.

## Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

The same principle applies to the building in reverse. When you zoom in from far away, the distance from the camera to the facade is large relative to the depth differences across the building, so the facade appears flattened/compressed. Nearby and distant elements look almost the same size, and the sense of depth is lost. When you walk close to the building and shoot without zoom the same building size, the camera is close enough that the near edge of the facade is closer than the far edge. This exaggerates the perspective; the near corner looks much larger than the far corner which gives a stronger sense of depth and makes the receding side of the building appear more sharply angled/tilted than it really is.
